# 2. Rules, submission and judging

Read on 2026-11-14 from the official Participant Guides (all dated November 13, 2026).

## Clock

| When (local time of our event) | What |
|--------------------------------|------|
| Sat Nov 14, 9:00 a.m. | Hackathon starts. Project tab appears on the team page. Submission opens. |
| **Sun Nov 15, 11:59 p.m.** | **Submission closes. Anything saved later is not judged.** |
| Dec 3, 2026 | Global Nominees announced |

For a Universal Event team the clock follows the Team Owner's local time.

The FAQ states that teams may not begin working on the challenge before the hackathon starts. Reading the resources and preparing accounts is preparation; building the solution starts at 9:00 a.m.

## Eligibility checklist

- [ ] Every member is registered, confirmed (not waitlisted) in the **same** Local Event, and listed on the team's Members tab.
- [ ] Team size: 1 to 6 for a Local Event. **A Universal Event team needs at least 2 members.**
- [ ] Project answers one official challenge.
- [ ] All required form fields are filled in.
- [ ] **Everything is in English**: the project page and every linked material. A demo video may be spoken in another language only with English subtitles. Slide decks must be in English.
- [ ] Demo respects the limit: video of 30 seconds or less, or a deck of 7 slides or fewer including title and intro.
- [ ] At least one NASA dataset or resource is used (needed for any Global Award).
- [ ] No images, voices or likeness of anyone under 18.
- [ ] No affiliation with China or a Chinese-owned entity in connection with the project (NASA policy review).
- [ ] "Submit for Judging" clicked before the deadline. Resubmitting is allowed; the last saved version counts.

## Project page fields

| Field | What to write |
|-------|---------------|
| Project Name | Name of the project |
| High-Level Project Summary | What we built, how it answers the challenge, why it matters |
| Link to Project Demo | Public link, no login: 30 s video or 7-slide deck |
| Link to Final Project | Public link, no login: website, repository or hosted files |
| Detailed Project Description | What it does, how it works, benefits, goals, tools and languages used |
| Use of Super Intelligence (SI) | Whether and how AI tools were used. Not shown to judges |
| NASA Data | The specific NASA data and resources used |
| Space Agency Partner Data and Other References | Every dataset, tool, code, text and image used, including open-source ones |

Practical details:

- Images can be uploaded (JPG, PNG, GIF up to 10 MB). Videos and documents cannot; link them from YouTube, Drive, GitHub or similar and test the link in a private window.
- If we host on a new domain, the guide recommends requesting a FortiGuard Web Filter classification so judges behind filters can open it: <https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit> (Company Name: "N/A").
- Use the edit "Lock" when editing; another member can still overwrite unsaved work.

## Rules on AI ("SI") use

- AI tools are allowed to speed up the work. They should not be the source of the idea for how to solve the challenge.
- Every use must be marked: generated images and videos carry a visible watermark; generated audio, code and data are acknowledged in descriptive text and metadata.
- Generated content may not use or modify NASA logos, flags or mission identifiers.
- Judges see those markers and weigh originality, intent and execution.

**Consequence for this repository:** we keep an `AI_USE.md` log and state AI assistance in the README, so the form field can be filled in from it.

## Licensing and citations (Terms and Conditions, updated August 7, 2026)

- Original content must be free of restrictions or under an OSI-approved open-source license. With no license stated, NASA distributes it under **Apache 2.0**.
- Non-NASA material (data or code) must be free and public or OSI-licensed, and its license must be named in our documentation. NASA will not publish a solution that lacks this.
- **All sources must be cited, even open ones.** Missing citations can cost a nomination or an award.
- No commercial promotion, no confidential data, no personally identifiable information.
- NASA may publish and redistribute the solution, and may use participants' names and likeness for outreach.

**Consequence for this repository:** a `LICENSE` file (Apache 2.0 unless the team chooses another OSI license), and a single references register with license and citation per source: [7. References](07-references.md).

## Judging criteria (each scored 1 to 5)

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | What it means for us |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|
| Impact | A significant problem, wide reach | Name the farmers served and the decision improved |
| Creativity | A novel approach or a meaningful improvement | The rotation comparison with evidence and gaps is our differentiator |
| Validity | Scientifically sound, feasible, usable | Published formulas (FAO-56), honest resolution limits, working prototype |
| Relevance | Directly answers the challenge; **NASA data is integral, not decorative** | NASA series must drive the scores, not sit in a side panel |
| Presentation | Clear, engaging storytelling in demo and project page | Plan the 30 s demo early and assign an owner |

The guide stresses that teams tend to neglect Presentation and asks for equal attention to it.

## Global Awards (ten)

Best Use of Science, Best Use of Data, Best Use of Technology, Galactic Impact, Best Mission Concept, Most Inspirational, Best Storytelling, Global Connection, Art & Technology, Local Impact.

The closest fits for a rotation tool piloted with real farmers are **Local Impact**, **Best Use of Data** and **Best Use of Science**.
